Definitions, sources and data notes

Inflation adjustment

The dollar switch at the top of the page restates every dollar amount in FY 2024-25 dollars, the latest year with actual results: spending, adopted budgets, cost per pupil and per-student amounts, salaries, revenue and aid, fund balances, and each town's school levy. Rows tagged "$ adjusts" change in the chart and the label; rows tagged "Dollar details adjust" chart a share or rate, which inflation does not change, but the dollar amounts in their hover details do change. Two indexes from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics are offered: the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, Northeast region, all items (CUUR0100SA0), and the Employment Cost Index for total compensation of state and local government workers in educational services (CIU3016100000000I). Both are averaged July to June to match the fiscal year. CPI-U measures what households pay; the ECI measures what employers pay for labor, which is most of a school budget, but it is a national series and not specific to New Hampshire. The two give nearly the same answer over this period, and the US city average CPI-U (CUUR0000SA0) gives a slightly larger adjustment. October 2025 CPI was not collected, so FY 2025-26 CPI averages 11 months. FY 2026-27 adjustments use only the four months of CPI and one quarter of ECI published so far and should be read as estimates. Not adjusted: shares, rates, test results and counts; the property value behind each student, which is a market price rather than spending (tagged "Not adjusted"); and the local tax rate per $1,000, which is already a ratio. Each town's school levy is adjusted as a dollar amount, but the levy per $1,000 of value is not.

Spending

Total recurring expenditures from the district's DOE-25 Annual Financial Report filed with the NH Department of Education: current operating spending, excluding facility construction and bond principal. Total expenditures including construction are in each year's tooltip; FY 2020-21 and FY 2021-22 include large middle school construction outlays funded by the 2020 bond. FY 2025-26 actuals appear after the district files its report, so that year shows the adopted budget.

Adopted budget

The operating budget article as printed on the March ballot, all funds, approved by voters for the following school year. FY 2026-27 was approved 1,897 to 1,110 on March 10, 2026. Every year from FY 2017-18 to FY 2023-24 was checked against the budget tables in the district's official annual reports.

Students enrolled

October 1 fall enrollment (NH Department of Education), as printed in the district's 2026 Annual Report. No FY 2026-27 district-wide projection has been sourced yet.

Budget per student

Adopted budget divided by October 1 enrollment. FY 2026-27 divides by the latest enrollment on file (FY 2025-26).

Actual cost per pupil

The NH DOE's current expenditure per pupil from the DOE-25 District Profile, an actual-spending figure that excludes construction, bond principal and similar items. It is shown for the district overall; the tooltip adds the elementary, middle and high school figures.

Share spent on instruction

Regular education, special programs, vocational and other instructional programs as a share of total recurring expenditures. Other categories are student and staff support, administration and business services, plant operations, transportation, debt interest and food service. Debt interest rose from $0.3 million to about $1.4 million after the 2020 bond, which lowers every other category's share a little.

Local property tax share

Local property tax as a share of total revenues in the DOE-25. State aid is adequacy aid plus other state aid. Federal aid and tuition, food service and other local revenue make up the rest.

Reading, math and science

Share of tested students scoring at level 3 or 4 on the NH statewide assessments, all tested grades, regular denominator (students tested), from the NH DOE disaggregated assessment files. Science is tested in a few grades only. No results were published for 2019-20. Participation in 2020-21 was 89% in the district and 80% statewide, so that year is less comparable. The 2017-18 file reports several test types in the same year (including alternate and SAT-based tests for some grades), so comparisons with later years should be made with care.

Benefits load

Employee benefits divided by salaries for instructional accounts 1100 to 1400 in the DOE-25 Salaries-Benefits sheet. It covers benefits the district pays on instructional staff (health, retirement and similar) and does not include administration or support staff. The FY 2024-25 sheet breaks salaries and benefits out by teachers, aides and other staff for the first time in the files reviewed, and benefits rose about 10% that year while salaries were flat, so the one-year jump to 50.4% should be confirmed with the district before conclusions are drawn.

Federal aid

Federal aid received, from the DOE-25 District Profile revenue section. Totals include pandemic-era programs in some years and recurring programs such as Title and IDEA funds in all years.

Student growth

Mean student growth percentile (SGP) from the NH DOE assessment files: a student's improvement compared with students who started at a similar level. 50 is typical. No growth figure was found for 2020-21, and none exists for 2019-20.

Reading for student groups

Share proficient among tested students with an IEP (individualized education program) and among economically disadvantaged students. These groups are small, so the lines move more than the all-student results.

Attendance

District attendance rate (share of half-days attended, all grades) from the NH DOE district attendance files, 2017-18 to 2021-22. Later years are published through the DOE's iPlatform report viewer, which returned server errors on every attempt in November 2026. The district's annual report cards print attendance by school level through 2024-25, but the 2022-23 and 2023-24 columns are identical and 2024-25 jumps to about 98% for both the district and the state, which looks like a change in how it is measured, so those years are not plotted.

Local school tax rate

The local education tax rate per $1,000 of assessed value (and, in the tooltip, the total rate) for Durham, Lee and Madbury, tax years 2017 to 2025 from the NH Department of Education's "Valuations, Property Tax Assessments and Tax Rates of School Districts" files, and tax year 2026 from the Department of Revenue Administration's posted rates. Tax years 2020 to 2025 were checked against the Department of Revenue Administration's municipal rate sheets and agree. Each rate is shown against the school year it funds (tax year 2025 against FY 2025-26). A town's rate depends on its share of the district's cost and its own assessed value, so it moves with revaluations as well as with spending: Durham's and Madbury's drops in some years reflect new valuations, not lower spending. Lee's 2026 rate had not been set when this page was compiled.

Who pays among the three towns, and the property value behind each student

Both rows come from the same DOE files. The school levy is the "local education tax assessed for schools" for each town; the three towns add up to the district total in every year, and the total equals the local property tax revenue in the district's DOE-25 for every year from FY 2017-18 to FY 2024-25. Equalized value is the state's estimate of full market value including utilities, which makes towns comparable after revaluations. Property value per student divides the three towns' equalized value by October 1 enrollment and is not adjusted for inflation. The tooltip also shows each town's share of the district's equalized value, so a town's share of the levy can be compared with its share of the tax base. The state's own education property tax (SWEPT) is a separate assessment and is not part of the school levy shown here.

Per-student measures

Special programs (function 1200, which includes special education), plant operations, transportation, bond interest, local property tax and state aid from the DOE-25 District Profile, each divided by October 1 enrollment. Cost per pupil by school level is the NH DOE figure for elementary, middle and high school. Administration and business is the sum of general administration and business (2300, 2800), school administration (2400) and business services (2500) as a share of operating spending. Instruction salaries and benefits are the DOE-25 Salaries-Benefits totals for accounts 1100 to 1400. All dollar rows respond to the inflation toggle.

State adequacy aid

New Hampshire pays "adequacy aid" through a formula: a base amount per pupil plus extra for students who qualify for free or reduced meals, special education and English learners. The state also levies the Statewide Education Property Tax (SWEPT) in every town; each town keeps SWEPT up to the formula cost, so the total state grant is SWEPT the towns keep plus an adequacy grant on top. Oyster River's three towns are calculated separately and summed here. From 2017-18 to 2024-25 the sum matches the adequacy aid in the district's DOE-25 to the dollar. The grant can exceed the formula cost because of hold-harmless and targeted grants. Figures for 2025-26 and 2026-27 are the state's estimates; the district has not filed those years. Formula cost per pupil uses state membership counts, which differ slightly from October 1 enrollment.

Cost per pupil vs. New Hampshire

The state average is the DOE's published average for district-operated schools. The median and rank are computed from the same DOE file among the 52 to 54 districts that report elementary, middle and high school costs. Comparing districts with different grade ranges or very different sizes is rough; the DOE itself cautions that only total figures should be compared.

Teacher pay and staffing

Starting salary is the lowest step on the district's salary schedule for a teacher with a bachelor's degree and no experience; the New Hampshire figure is the DOE's unweighted average of the districts that reported. Average teacher salary and the student-teacher ratio are the DOE's published district figures; the NH values are the DOE's state average and state total. Two Oyster River average-salary values are not plotted because they look unreliable: the 2022-23 file lists $63,538, about 17% below the years on either side, and the 2024-25 file repeats the 2023-24 figure of $80,573 to the dollar even though the teacher count changed. Both are shown in the tooltip. The ratio files stop at 2021-22; later years exist only in the DOE's iPlatform viewer. The DOE's student and teacher counts for the ratio differ from October 1 enrollment (for example, 2,028 students in 2017-18 against 2,147 elsewhere on this page).

General fund balance

The general fund balance at June 30 from the district's audited financial statements, total and unassigned. Committed money is set aside for specific purposes, such as the facilities and special education trust funds and encumbrances. Unassigned balance can be used to lower the next tax rate, and state law lets a district keep it only if voters have adopted that authority. The FY 2024-25 audit is not posted yet. The district's 2021 annual report reprinted the FY 2018-19 audit instead of the FY 2019-20 one, so the FY 2019-20 balance comes from the audit report on orcsd.org.

Enrollment by level, special education, lunch eligibility and English learners

Level counts are the October 1 history the NH DOE prints in the district's annual report. From 2018-19 on the DOE district total is 25 to 34 students higher than the four levels add up to, and the DOE does not itemize them. Special education counts come from the district's special education office as of October 1 and include preschoolers and out-of-district placements; the share divides by October 1 enrollment, so it is approximate. Lunch eligibility and English learner counts are DOE figures for October 1, shown as a share of enrollment.

Education Freedom Accounts

Students who live in Durham, Lee or Madbury and hold an Education Freedom Account, from the DOE's by-municipality reports dated June 2022, June 2023, April 2024, March 2025 and March 2026, with the state cost of an adequate education for them in the tooltip. The first two years count students in proportion to the part of the year they were enrolled, so the early figures are small and not strictly comparable. The accounts are paid by the state, not by the district, and not every account holder left an Oyster River school.

Class size, SAT scores and graduates' plans

Class size is the average for grades 1 and 2 and grades 3 and 4 at Mast Way and Moharimet as the DOE reports it; the DOE lists none for the middle school, and Oyster River is the simple average of the four figures. SAT scores are the average for Oyster River High School test takers, reading and writing plus math, with New Hampshire's average beside them; the national averages printed in the report are left out because the 2019 figures do not match the College Board's. Which students test changes from year to year. Plans after graduation are the share of all students who completed high school that year, as the DOE reports it, a year or more later.

More detail

Dropouts are counts of students who left during the year or did not return by October 2; with one or two a year they are too small to trend. Career technology counts Oyster River High School students at the Tri-City Career Technology Centers. Teacher degrees and experience are shares and counts of professional staff (teachers, guidance, special education, library and nursing). Attendance by school level is averaged across kindergarten, elementary, middle and high school from the DOE tables in the district report. The district's report prints identical figures for 2022-23 and 2023-24, and the 2024-25 figures jump to about 98% for both the district and the state, so only 2017-18 to 2022-23 are drawn. Enrollment projections come from the district's Long Range Planning Committee (2018 to 2024 reports) and NESDEC (2021 to 2024 reports); each year shows the latest projection printed before that school year began, and the 2025 and 2026 reports print none. The committee's one-year-ahead figures were close (2,175 for 2019-20 against 2,168 actual, 2,115 for 2021-22 against 2,130), but the longer-range figures drifted: NESDEC projected 2,139 for 2024-25 against 2,097 actual and 2,149 for 2025-26 against 2,038, while the committee's lower figures were 2,015 and 1,988. Staff experience counts start in 2017-18, the first year printed in the reports used.

How Durham, Lee and Madbury share the school bill

The NH DOE's list of apportionment formulas says Oyster River divides its total operating budget 50% on average daily membership (students living in each town) and 50% on equalized property value, and credits adequacy aid to the towns. The district's annual reports (2018 to 2024) print each town's resulting percentage, the state grant and state education tax subtracted from it, and the net amount to raise. A 50/50 average of the students and value shares in the DOE's equalized valuation per pupil files reproduces those official percentages to within 0.01 of a point (FY 2017-18 to 2021-22 and 2023-24), using the latest file available when the budget was set, which runs two to three years behind the budget year. The 2025 and 2026 reports no longer print the split, so FY 2024-25 and 2025-26 are taken from the NH DOE cooperative apportionment sheets that the Department of Revenue Administration publishes (official 51.86 / 32.67 / 15.47 and 52.09 / 32.99 / 14.92 for Durham, Lee and Madbury). The FY 2026-27 figure is an estimate from the newest DOE file; the split approved on October 30, 2026 was not in the board materials found, and it moves back toward Durham because Durham's property value rose faster than Lee's and Madbury's in the 2024 file.

School tax per $1,000 divides each town's net school levy by its equalized value, which removes differences in how recently each town revalued. The school share of the tax rate adds the state education rate to the local school rate and divides by the total rate (town, county, local school and state school); rates are set on assessed value, so a revaluation can move them without any change in spending. State credits are each town's adequacy grant plus the state education property tax it keeps. Levy per resident student divides the net levy by the town's students in the DOE file the formula used. The measures answer different questions: on property wealth and on the share of the tax rate that pays for schools Lee has been at or near the top since 2024-25, while per student Durham's taxpayers contribute the most because it has the largest tax base.

State building aid, special education aid and the school safety data

The NH DOE's Other State Aid Programs page lists the payments beyond adequacy aid. For Oyster River the building aid schedule is the plainest: $523,742 a year from 2012-13 through 2022-23, then $0 from 2023-24 onward, which removes about a half million dollars of state money that was helping to pay the school bonds. Special education aid pays part of the cost of students whose services cost more than 3.5 times the state average per pupil; the amount owed to Oyster River ranged from $64,621 (2020-21) to $303,788 (2022-23), and the state sometimes pays less than the amount owed because the appropriation is prorated. Each year's aid is based on the previous school year's costs. Smaller programs are shown in the special education aid tooltip: career technology transportation aid ($1,966 to $14,044 a year) and the one-time Education Freedom Account phase-out grant ($6,627 in 2022-23, $10,067 in 2024-25 and $9,617 in 2025-26). Charter school aid, lease aid and kindergarten aid in the same DOE files do not list Oyster River.

The school climate rows come from the DOE's annual School Safety Data Collection, which each district files by school. The page adds the Mast Way, Moharimet, middle and high school figures (plus the preschool program where it is listed). The counts are small, they depend on how each school records an event, and a few single years (the 34 drug incidents in 2017-18, the 165 truant students in 2020-21, the 34 restraint reports in 2022-23) are better read as questions to ask the district than as trends. The same files also give each school's October 1 enrollment and, for 2015-16 to 2019-20, harassment and discrimination reports and the section on serious crimes, which is zero in every year; these are in the files linked below but are not charted.

Not yet included

District attendance and chronic absenteeism after 2022-23 are not on the page: the DOE’s iExplore and iReport dashboards do not carry them, the older iPlatform report is retired, and the district’s own reports repeat or re-base the later figures. The FY 2024-25 audit is not on the district’s audit page, the district’s special education spending detail is not charted, and SAT results for 2026 and Lee’s 2026 tax rate were not yet published when this page was compiled. Other files from the DOE and the Department of Revenue Administration (principal salaries, indirect cost rates, kindergarten aid, AP district maps, equalization surveys) are kept in the project’s raw folder but are not charted.

Data notes

  • Enrollment follows the NH DOE's district total (iExplore). For 2025-26 that is 2,064; the district's annual report prints 2,038 in its history table and 2,040 in its school-by-school count. The state-first choice and every other district-versus-state difference are listed in CONFLICTS.md in the repo.
  • The FY 2026-27 ballot states an RSA 189:75 average cost per pupil of $24,268 for the preceding year; the DOE-25 District Profile for 2024-25 shows $23,922. The two are calculated differently.
  • Adopted budgets for FY 2017-18 to FY 2023-24 were checked against the operating budget totals in the district's annual reports. The FY 2021-22 figure is the $50,173,045 on that year's warrant; the following year's report restates it as $50,249,667 in its comparison table.
  • State-reported adequacy aid is sometimes revised after the fact. The 2025-26 and 2026-27 figures are estimates and may change.
  • Revenue and spending function figures are available through FY 2024-25 only; FY 2025-26 will appear once the district files.
